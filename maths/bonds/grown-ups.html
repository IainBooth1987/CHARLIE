Charlie's Learning · Number bonds — a guide for grown-ups

What are number bonds?

A number bond is a pair of numbers that make another number — 3 and 2 make 5. Teachers call the bigger idea composition: every number is made up of smaller numbers.

Parts and a whole
When 5 is split into 3 and 2, the 3 is a part, the 2 is a part, and 5 is the whole.
All the ways to make 5
0 and 5 · 1 and 4 · 2 and 3 (and the other way round). Knowing these "by heart" makes adding and taking away much easier later.

What's expected by the end of Reception?

The Early Learning Goal for Number says children will have a deep understanding of numbers to 10, including the composition of each number, and will automatically recall number bonds to 5 (including taking-away facts) and some to 10, including doubles.

The four games

GameWhat your child doesWhat it builds
🙈 How Many Hiding?Sees the whole group, then some hide (in the cave, the kennel, behind the planet…). "3 are out — how many are hiding?"The classic early-years hiding game: knowing the missing part from the whole. Wholes of 3–4, then 5, then 6–7, then 10.
🍒 Part, Part, WholeThe "cherry" model — the whole at the top, two parts below, one missing.The same picture schools use. Later levels sometimes ask for the whole instead.
🎨 Two-Colour FrameSome are already in (blue). Add more (orange) to make the number.Making a number from two parts on a five or ten frame — all the ways to make 5, then 10.
🦋 DoublesA mirror shows the same on both sides. "Double 3 is…?"Double facts to 10.

After each answer the game says the bond out loud — "3 and 2 make 5" — because hearing and saying the facts helps them stick.

How you can help

While they play

💬 Use the words: "One part is 3, the other part is 2, the whole is 5."
  • If they count all the objects one by one, that's fine at first — the aim is that they start to just know.
  • Ask "Is there another way to make 5?"

Away from the screen

  • Hide and peek: put 5 toys under a tea towel, take some out — "how many are still hiding?"
  • Two hands: hold 5 small things, split them between your hands — "3 in this hand, so how many in the other?"
  • Egg boxes and ten frames: fill an egg box with two colours of pasta or pom-poms.
  • Doubles everywhere: legs on a chair (2 and 2), ladybird spots, socks, dominoes.
