Charlie's Learning · Shapes & patterns — a guide for grown-ups

Why shapes and patterns?

Spotting patterns is at the heart of maths. Children who can see what repeats, and say what comes next, are learning to look for rules — the same skill they will later use with numbers. Shape work builds the language of maths (sides, corners, straight, round) and spatial reasoning.

Shapes
Development Matters asks children to talk about shapes such as circles, rectangles and triangles using words like sides, corners, straight, flat and round — and to select, turn and put shapes together.
Patterns
Children copy and continue simple AB patterns, then spot and fix a mistake, then move on to patterns like ABB and ABC — and say which part repeats.

The four games

GameWhat your child doesWhat it builds
🔍 Shape Hunt"Find the triangle!" Later levels turn and stretch the shapes, then ask "find the shape with 3 corners".Knowing a triangle is still a triangle when it's upside down, long and thin, or small. Using sides and corners to decide. The corners light up after each answer.
➡️ What Comes Next?Carries on a pattern of the world's pictures. Level 4 has a gap in the middle.Continuing AB, ABB and ABC patterns. The repeating part is shaded afterwards and the pattern is read out loud.
🔧 Spot the MistakeFinds the one that breaks the pattern and taps it.Following the research order: an extra item first, then a wrong item, then harder patterns. If they get stuck, the game tracks the pattern from the start, one at a time.
🧱 Shape PicturesBuilds a house, rocket, truck or castle by choosing the shape that fits each flashing space.A shape can be made of other shapes, and shapes can be turned to fit (the rocket's fins are triangles turned round).

Squares and rectangles: a square is really a special kind of rectangle, so the game never asks children to "find the rectangle" with a square in the line-up.

How you can help

💬 Say patterns out loud together: "red, blue, red, blue…" — clap, stamp, clap, stamp works too.
  • Shape hunts at home: the door is a rectangle, the clock is a circle, a slice of toast cut corner to corner makes two triangles.
  • Turn it round: cut out a paper triangle and turn it upside down — "Is it still a triangle? How do you know?" (count the corners).
  • Make a mistake on purpose: lay out a pattern of spoons and forks with one wrong, and let them fix it.
  • Build with blocks: bricks, LEGO and shape blocks — "What shapes did you use for your house?"
