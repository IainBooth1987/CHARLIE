Matching what happens at school

Charlie's school teaches phonics with Little Wandle Letters and Sounds Revised. These games follow the same order of sounds and tricky words, week by week, so practice at home matches what was taught in class. Choose the level your child has reached at school.

Each level also mixes in sounds from earlier levels, because going back over them is how they stick. Every word in the games only uses sounds taught up to that level.

The four games

GameWhat your child doesWhy
👂 Sound SpotterLooks at a picture, hears the word, and picks the sound at the start, middle or end.Linking each sound to the letter or letters that spell it.
📖 Read ItTaps the sound buttons under the word, says each sound, blends them, then picks the matching picture.Blending is reading. The word is not read out until they've chosen, so they have to read it themselves.

Sound buttons: dots and lines

Under each word is a dot for a sound made by one letter, and a line where two or three letters make one sound (like sh or igh). Point to each one and say the sound, then sweep along to blend: sh – i – p … ship.

💬 Say pure sounds: "mmm" not "muh", "sss" not "suh". Short sounds like t and p are quick and crisp. Little Wandle's own parent videos show exactly how each sound is said.

About the voice

There is no computer voice anywhere on the site. Computer voices say letter names ("ess", "tee") rather than pure sounds, which muddles children. Instead, children see the letters and say the sounds themselves, exactly as in class. In Tricky Words, your child reads the word aloud and you tap ✅ Read it!, or 💡 Show the tricky part if they need help. If real recordings are added later, the sounds and words play automatically and Tricky Words switches to "listen and find".

How you can help

  • Little and often: one round a day is plenty.
  • Let them say it first: in Read It, wait for them to sound out before helping.
  • Sound-spotting walks: "I spy something that starts with sss…"
  • Use the school's books: the reading book they bring home is matched to what they know. Read it more than once — the first time to decode, the next to read smoothly.
